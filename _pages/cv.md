MARKET REACTION TO FOMC MEETINGS | INDEPENDENT RESEARCH PAPER | JUNE-AUGUST 2026
  * Conducted an event study of market reactions to 150 FOMC announcements (2008–2026), analyzing effects on equities
(S&P 500, Russell 2000, XLF), bond yields (2Y–30Y), FX (DXY), commodities (gold, silver), and the volatility index (VIX)
across a [−1, +1] business-day window—conducted deep research and analysis of changes in security prices, expected
returns, abnormal performance, and investment opportunities during turbulent and uncertain interest rate environments.
  * Built a Python pipeline (Pandas, NumPy, Scipy, Matplotlib) that ingests data from the FRED API and Yahoo Finance,
constructs a cross-validated FOMC calendar classifying each meeting as hike/cut/hold, and computes cumulative abnormal
returns using a constant-mean model over a [−120, −20] estimation window.
  * Applied one-sample and Welch's t-tests with bootstrap confidence intervals to test reactions across policy regimes (GFC,
ZIRP, tightening, easing) and separated scheduled from emergency meetings to address endogeneity between crisis
conditions and Fed regime action—analysis of hundreds of data outputs, demonstrating strong attention to detail
  * Found significant Treasury yield reactions across all decision types (2Y: p<0.01), consistent with forward guidance
repricing, while equities showed weak unconditional responses in line with the efficient-markets hypothesis (Kuttner,
2001)—concluded that treasury markets are the most effected by unexpected policies—full paper found on website.
